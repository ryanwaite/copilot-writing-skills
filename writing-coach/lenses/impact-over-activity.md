# Lens: Impact over Activity

**Concern:** The draft narrates *what the author did* instead of *what it changed*. It "reads the news" (a chronological list of activity) and buries or omits the outcome, decision, or so-what that the reader actually needs.

## When it applies

Status reports, weekly updates, project summaries, standups, brag docs, exec briefings, retros: anywhere a busy reader is asking "so what?" and "what do you need from me?"

## When it does not apply

Writing where the activity *is* the deliverable: meeting minutes, run logs, audit trails, changelogs, procedural how-tos, or a record kept precisely so someone can reconstruct the sequence of events. In those, chronological narration is the point, not a defect.

## Signals to detect

- **Activity verbs with no result**: "worked on," "met with," "looked into," "attended," "reviewed," "discussed," "started," "continued," "spent time on," with nothing after them about the effect.
- **Chronological narration**: "First I… then I… after that I…", where sequence stands in for significance.
- **Effort as the point**: hours, meetings, and volume presented as accomplishment ("had 6 syncs," "spent all week on").
- **Buried lede**: the one thing the reader needs (a decision, a risk, a number, an ask) sits in the middle or the last line instead of the front.
- **No so-what**: a true statement of activity that never connects to a goal, a metric, a decision, or a consequence.
- **Missing the ask**: the update implies the reader should do something, but never says what.

## The coaching move

For each activity statement, ask the **"so what?" chain** until it lands on impact:

> *did X* → so what? → *which caused Y* → so what? → *which matters because Z (decision / metric / risk / dollar / user)*

Reframe so the **outcome leads** and the activity becomes supporting detail (or gets cut). A good test: could the author have done twice as much work and had the update read identically? If yes, it's measuring activity, not impact.

Preserve real activity that a reader needs (e.g. "met with Legal" matters if the *outcome* was a decision). Cut activity that only signals busyness. Surface the **ask** explicitly if one is implied.

This lens often *cuts* length while the specificity lens *adds* detail. They reconcile: **trade activity narration for specific outcomes.** Fewer words, more signal.

Do not invent an impact the author never stated. If the outcome is missing, ask for it. An activity report with no known outcome is itself a finding ("what came of this?").

## Questions to ask

- "So what? What changed because you did this?"
- "What decision does this update enable, or what do you need from the reader?"
- "If you'd done nothing this week, what would be different in this report?"
- "What's the one line an exec would need, and is it at the top?"
- "This meeting: what was decided or unblocked?"

## Example

**Before**
> This week I spent a lot of time on the vendor evaluation. I set up calls with three vendors, put together a comparison spreadsheet, and reviewed their security docs. I also synced with procurement twice and started drafting a recommendation.

**Coaching (what the lens produces)**
- Entire paragraph is activity: calls, spreadsheet, syncs, drafting. *No recommendation, no decision, no ask.*
- Buried lede: the reader wants the recommendation, not the process. *Which vendor, and why?*
- "started drafting a recommendation": *what does it say? When will they have it?*

**Author supplied** (facts the author provided when answering; not invented by the coach)
> Recommendation is Vendor B; B is the only one meeting SOC 2 and is 20% under budget; A fails security review; C is out of range; sign-off needed from procurement by Friday to hit the Q3 start; wants approval or concerns by Thursday.

**After the author answers**
> Recommend we go with Vendor B: it's the only one that meets our SOC 2 requirement and comes in 20% under budget. Vendor A is cheaper but fails security review; Vendor C is out of range. I need sign-off from procurement by Friday to hit the Q3 start date. Can you approve or flag concerns by Thursday?
